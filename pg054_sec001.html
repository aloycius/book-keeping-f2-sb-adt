Chapter Two

Correction of Book-keeping errors and the Trial balance

Introduction

Books of accounts may contain errors. Book-keeping errors may originate from the source documents, from entries in the books of prime entry, ledger posting, or at any other stage in the accounting cycle. In this chapter, you will learn errors and the correction of errors in the trial balance. The competencies developed will enable you to make journal entries and relevant ledger postings to correct errors and prepare an adjusted trial balance.

Making errors when preparing the trial balance

Errors in the trial balance

Every transaction made by the business is supposed to be entered and posted using the double entry system. The amount entered for each transaction must match with a respective source document. Further, each ledger account is supposed to be appropriately balanced, and the general ledger balances are normally summarised in the trial balance from time to time.

A trial balance requires that debit and credit balances must be equal. However, some errors may occur due to mistakes

made by book-keepers in recording, posting, or totalling. This may lead to discrepancies between debits and credits. Therefore, all errors must be identified and corrected.

Meaning of book-keeping errors Book-keep ing errors are mistakes committed at any stage in the book- keeping process.
