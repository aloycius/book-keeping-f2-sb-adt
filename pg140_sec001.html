Approaches to preparing a budget

There are various approaches of preparing
a budget, depending on the specific needs
of a business. Each approach has its
advantages and challenges. Some of the
common budgeting approaches include:

(i) Incremental budgeting
:This method
uses the previous budget as a starting
point and adjusts based on expected
changes. For example, if a business
spent TZS 1,000,000 on rent last year
and the rent increases by 5%, the
new budget will include the updated
rent cost of TZS 1,050,000. This
approach is simple and easy to use
because it does not require complex
calculations. However, it may use

previous spending patterns without
checking if they are still necessary.

Furthermore, it does not encourage
cost-cutting or finding new ways
to save money, which can lead to
inefficiencies over time.

(ii) Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB): This approach starts from zero. Every

expense must be justified before it
is included in the budget. Previous
costs are not automatically carried
forward; each department or expense
must prove its necessity. This method
helps to reduce wasteful spending
because businesses carefully examine
all expenses before approving them.

However, it requires more time and
effort to prepare since managers must
justify every cost, making it a more
detailed and demanding process.

Despite this challenge, ZBB ensures
that resources are allocated efficiently
and only necessary expenses are
included.

(iii)Activity-based-budgeting
:This
budgeting method focuses on the
activities that generate value for the
business rather than just previous
expenses. The budget is developed
by identifying key activities and
estimating how much they cost.

For example, if a business plans
to increase customer engagement,
it may allocate more money to
marketing and customer service
activities. A business may decide to
spend TZS 3,000,000 on advertising
if it expects this will increase sales.

This

approach

helps

businesses

to spend money more effectively.
