Student’s Book Form Two

The name imprest is historically associated
with a Latin word ‘imprestare’, meaning

‘to lend’. Imprest sytem works not only
for processing petty cash transactions, but
also other business for larger transactions.

The imprest system for petty cash is
the system in which the petty cashier
periodically receives a specified amount
of cash from chief cashier. The petty
cashier will be required to maintain all
the documents (vouchers and receipts)
that evidence payment from such amount
and the amount to be restored by the chief
cashier. In other words, the chief cashier

‘lends’ the petty cashier a specific amount
of cash for use in various cash transactions.

It remains as an imprest (loan) until
evidence in terms of documents (petty
cash vouchers and receipts) is submitted
to the chief cashier to justify the payments.

Ideally, the petty cashier will have to
refund the money to the chief cashier
when he or she fails to provide evidence

(source documents) for the expenditure.

Traditionally, many businesses use the
petty cash voucher (abbreviated as PCV)
as a source document for payments from
the petty cash, although the PCV can
be complemented with receipts or other
documents from third parties.

Usefulness of petty cash book and the
imprest system

The use of petty cash book and the imprest
system is important for the following
reasons:

(a)

It assists in the regulation of petty

expenses. This is because each petty

payment will be associated with a

petty cash voucher that is backed up

with evidence for the payment.

(b)

It enables easy detection of errors. If

there is an error in petty payments,

it is detected within the petty cash

system. This would have been

difficult to detect when all payments

are handled through the main cash

book.

(c)

It reduces burden to the chief cashier,

who would have been responsible to

record every tiny payment.

(d)

It helps in detection of fraud

(improper use of cash). This is

because the chief cashier can verify

the petty cash book with the petty

cash vouchers and other relevant

supporting documents.

(e)

It saves time in posting transactions.

This is because the petty expenses are

batched up and posted periodically,

for example, weekly to the respective

ledger accounts.

(f)

It helps in training junior accounting

staff to handle cash responsibilities.
