Correction of Book-keeping errors and the Trial balance

Exercise 2.3

A review of the books of Mkude for the year ended 31 st December 2024 revealed the following book-keeping errors:

(i) Purchase of a machine costing TZS 15,000,000 for use in the business had been debited to the purchases account.

(ii) Goods purchased from C. Majaliwa

for TZS 5,000,000 were credited to the account of G. Majaliwa.

(iii) A sales transaction of goods to Mangi for TZS 1,850,000 was entered in the sales day book as TZS 1,580,000.

(iv) The salaries and wages account was over added by TZS 75,000 and rent income account had also been over added by TZS 75,000.

(v) An invoice from Amina for TZS 540,000 in respect of goods purchased has been omitted from the books.

Required:

(a) For each given data, write the type of book-keeping error.

(b) Show the journal entries to correct the book-keeping errors.

Correction of book-keeping errors that affect trial balance agreement These errors are unilateral; meaning that the error is made in one of the accounts involved in recording the transaction. In case, it is a casting error in balancing an account, the entry to correct will be crediting (provided that the casting error increased debit balance or reduced a credit balance). The vice versa is also true.

The entry required to correct an error which affects trial balance does not necessarily require double entry as was the case with errors that do not affect trial balance agreement. Instead, the corresponding entries for these errors are made in a temporary account opened for special purpose. This account is called a suspense account.

Suspense Account

A suspense account is an account that is used to temporarily record a balance that cannot be explained at a particular moment. Traditionally, a suspense account is used by businesses as part of general ledger to record unclear entries that require clarification. A good example is when a business notices a credit entry has been made in its bank statement, meaning that someone has made a deposit in its account.
