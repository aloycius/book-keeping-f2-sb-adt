Determining the opening and closing

cash balances

Understanding the opening and closing

balances is crucial as it enables proper
cash management.

(i) Opening balance
. This is the cash
available at the beginning of the
budgeting period. It includes cash in
hand, money in the bank, or savings.

For example, if a tied shop expects
to have TZS 600,000 in cash at the
end of this (current) month, such
amount will be the opening balance
for the following (new) month. A
sufficient opening balance ensures
that a business operates smoothly and
reduces the risk of cash shortages.

(ii) Closing balance. This is the expected cash balance at the end of the budgeting period and is calculated using the formula:

Closing balance = Opening balance +

Net cash flow

Net cash flow is the difference between

total cash receipts and disbursements
during

the

budgeting

period.

For

example, if a boda boda rider expects
to start the month with TZS 150,000 to
earn TZS 500,000 from fares, and spend

TZS 300,000 on fuel, maintenance, and
personal expenses, the closing balance
will be:

TZS 150,000 + (TZS 500,000 - TZS

300,000) = TZS 350,000. A positive
closing

balance

means

there

will

be surplus to meet future financial
obligations, while a negative balance
indicates that disbursements are higher
than cash receipts, requiring better
financial planning.
