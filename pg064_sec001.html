Student’s Book Form Two

Correcting an error of principle In this case, one of the entries has been recorded in a wrong account that also happens to fall in a different class of accounts. The correct double entry should be made such that: (i) It cancels or offsets the entry recorded in the wrong account; and (ii) It records the transaction in the appropriate account. Example 2.3 demonstrates this correction.

Example 2.3

The business issued a TZS 7,000,000 cheque to purchase a Suzuki van for use in delivering goods to customers. This transaction was debited to the purchases account and credited to the bank account. To correct this error, the entry in the purchases account should be cancelled by crediting the purchases account. An entry should be made to record the transaction in the appropriate asset account, the delivery van account. This is done by debiting the delivery van account.

Journal Entry

Date Details Dr (TZS) Cr (TZS)
Delivery van 7,000,000
Purchases 7,000,000
Being error of principle now corrected

Correcting an error of original entry This aims to rectify the wrong original entry. If the figure recorded was higher than the correct amount, a double entry should be made to correct it. If on the other hand, the figure recorded is lower than the correct amount, an entry should be made to correct it. Since the accounts have an error of the same amount, the correct entry will be a double entry. The following two examples (2.4 and 2.5) illustrate errors of original entry and the respective corrections.

Example 2.4

A credit sale of goods TZS 380,000 to Ally Salehe was entered in the books as TZS 280,000. This means that the amount entered in both the trade receivables account and the sales account is TZS 100,000 lower than the correct amount. Double entry is necessary to correct the amount. Therefore, entries involving TZS 100,000 need to be made.
