Correcting an error of principle

In this case, one of the entries has been recorded in a wrong account that also happens
to fall in a different class of accounts. The correct double entry should be made such
that:

(i)

It cancels or offsets the entry recorded in the wrong account; and

(ii) It records the transaction in the appropriate account. Example 2.3 demonstrates
this correction.

Example 2.3

The business issued a TZS 7,000,000 cheque to purchase a Suzuki van for use in
delivering goods to customers. This transaction was debited to the purchases account
and credited to the bank account. To correct this error, the entry in the purchases
account should be cancelled by crediting the purchases account. An entry should
be made to record the transaction in the appropriate asset account, the delivery van
account. This is done by debiting the delivery van account.

Correcting an error of original entry

This aims to rectify the wrong original entry. If the figure recorded was higher than the
correct amount, a double entry should be made to correct it. If on the other hand, the
figure recorded is lower than the correct amount, an entry should be made to correct it.

Since the accounts have an error of the same amount, the correct entry will be a double
entry. The following two examples (2.4 and 2.5) illustrate errors of original entry and
the respective corrections.
