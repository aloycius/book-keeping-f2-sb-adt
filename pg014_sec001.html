The cheque is open because it does not have a crossing. This implies that the amount in the cheque can be paid to the payee in cash. The payee, however, still has an option to deposit the cheque in his/ her account.

On the other hand, a crossed cheque instructs the bank to deposit the amount stated on the cheque to the payee’s account, without the option for the drawee to pay the amount in cash to the payee.

Crossing is normally done by drawing or indicating two vertical lines on the face of the cheque. Some banks may add the words ‘A/C payee only’ which insist that the amount has to be deposited to the payee's account. For security purposes, most banks would by default have the cheques crossed, but give the drawer an option to ‘open’ the cheque by signing on the vertical lines crossing the cheque.
