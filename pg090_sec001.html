Student’s Book Form Two

Chapter summary 1. Book-keeping errors may occur by omission of entry, entering a wrong amount in the books of original entry, entering a transaction in the wrong book of original entry, wrong computation of ledger balances, or even wrong preparation of the trial balance.

2. The processes for discovering and identifying errors involve making sure that source documents exist for each transaction and are systematically arranged and filed to simplify tracing each transaction from the source document to the relevant journal. 3. There are two broad categories of errors, namely; errors that do not affect the trial balance agreement and errors that affect the trial balance agreement. 4. The errors that do not affect the trial balance agreement include error of omission, error of commission, error of principle, error of original entry, error of complete reversal of entries and compensating errors. 5. Identifying and correcting errors is crucial for maintaining accurate financial statements and ensuring the reliability of accounting records. 6. A suspense account is used by a business as a part of the general ledger to record unclear entries that require clarification.
