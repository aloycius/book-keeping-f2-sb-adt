Recording cash and banking transactions

Figure 1.4: Crossed Cheque with the words ‘A/C payee only’

Transacting using cheques is safe and convenient because the trader does not carry a lot of cash when making payments or collecting money from customers. The trader will be issuing cheques to suppliers and collecting cheques from customers. Banks normally keep records of transactions on each of the customers’ bank accounts. On the other hand, the customers will also have to make their own records of transactions with banks. Transactions made by the trader through cheques, and other transactions between the trader and the bank are recorded in a bank account.

Recording transactions in the bank account To a business, a bank account is a type of cash book in which transactions with a bank are recorded. It therefore applies the same rules of debit and credit that apply in recording transactions in the cash account. These are:

(a) Debit the bank account for transactions that increase the bank balance, for example, deposits of cheques. (b) Credit the bank account for transactions that decrease the bank balance, for example issue of cheques.

When a cheque is received by the business, there may be an option to present it for payment (cash collection) from the drawer’s bank or deposit the cheque in the business’ (payee’s) account, depending on the type of cheque. For the purpose of this book, the cheques received by the business, whether open or crossed, are deposited into the trader’s bank account.

To record a depositing of a cheque to the businesses` bank account, one will therefore debit the bank account and credit the drawer`s account in the ledger.

It should also be remembered that when the business operates more than one account (either with the same bank or with different banks), separate bank accounts will have to be maintained in the ledger for each such account.
