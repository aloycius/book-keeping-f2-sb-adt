Banking transactions refer to financial transactions that involve the flow of money into or out of a customer’s, or business’s bank account. This includes money deposits, transfers, withdrawal payments, and other financial transactions carried out in a bank account. For example, a customer deposits TZS 400,000 from weekly sales into the bank for personal savings, a company transfers TZS 800,000 using SimBanking for services received, and a business venture withdrawals TZS 9,000,000 from the bank for paying operating expenses. Mobile money transactions are treated as banking transactions because they have similar features.

Banking transactions provide a safe and secure way to handle money. They reduce the risk of loss or theft associated with holding cash. They also offer accurate record-keeping of transactions, which helps individuals and businesses to track their revenues and expenses effectively.

Cash book and its purpose

Cash book is a book used for recording the flow of cash into and out of the business. A cash book includes all accounts that record receipts and payments of cash for a business. Book-keeping practice recognises cash book as a book of original entry and as a ledger. It is therefore important to note that reference to cash book in this textbook is applicable to both, the case where the intended record is a journal and the case where it is a ledger. The purpose of the cash book is to ensure that up-to-date records of cash status are available at any point in time. When the cash status is readily available, it assists the management of the business to:

(a) properly plan for different transactions. (b) know the financial position (liquidity) of the business. (c) decide on whether they can make business expansion. (d) decide on whether they need a loan from external sources.
