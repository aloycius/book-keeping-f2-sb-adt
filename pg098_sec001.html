Importance of bank reconciliation statement

The importance of preparing bank reconciliation statement can be summarised in the following points:

(i) It helps in identifying possible errors in transactions. These include errors in the cash book and in the bank statement.

(ii) It helps for the business to have an updated cash book and determine the bank balance to be reported in the financial statements.

(iii) It is useful in tracking receipts such as direct credits, and expenses such as bank charges, penalties and update the ledger accordingly.

(iv) It may assist in discovering ‘difficulty’ customers. Such customers might have a tendency to delay payment through technicalities, such as issuing post-dated or erroneous cheques. It is possible to discover this when a business experiences and investigates a tendency of dishonoured cheques from one customer.

(v) It helps to detect fraud, for example stolen cheques cashed without

the knowledge of the account holder, or altered cheques. To get maximum benefits of this, the bank reconciliation process should be done by a different person other than the cashier who is responsible for handling cheques.

(vi) Bank reconciliation is also a required standard control procedure or activity for cash management.

Bank statement

This is a statement that shows transactions between the bank and its customer for a particular period as recorded by the bank. It is traditionally issued monthly by the bank, but may be issued at different intervals or at a specific date on customer’s request. The bank statement has columns for date, details, debit, credit and balance. The debit column of the bank statement reflects withdrawals, and credit column reflects deposits; it is opposite to the cash book. The balance column shows a balance of the customer’s account after every transaction. In other words, the balance is updated in real time after every transaction.
