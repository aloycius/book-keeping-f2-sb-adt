Detection of book-keeping errors

How does a business know that there are
errors in its records? There are important
steps to be taken to ensure that errors are
discovered.

Errors can be identified through the
following process:

(i)

Making sure that source documents

exist for each transaction and are

systematically arranged and filed.

(ii)

Tracing each transaction from source

documents to the relevant journal.

(iii) Vouching each journal entry to the
source documents.

(iv) Verifying every ledger entry with originating journal entry.

(v)

Tracing every journal entry to ledger

accounts.

(vi) Verifying balances in the ledger accounts.

(vii) Extracting a trial balance.

Consult your school accountant or
bursar, ask how they detect errors
when recording financial transactions.

Correction of errors in the trial

balance

A trial balance is an essential tool for
validating the accuracy of financial
records. It ensures that total debit and
credit balances are equal. However,
errors can still occur due to omissions,
incorrect postings, or miscalculations
and classifying accounts incorrectly.

Identifying and correcting errors is
crucial for maintaining accurate financial
statements and ensuring the reliability of
accounting records.

Correction of errors by journal

entries

When correcting book-keeping errors in
the trial balance using journal entries, the
process depends on whether such errors
affect the trial balance or not. Correction
of errors which do not affect trial balance
differ from those affecting trial balance.
