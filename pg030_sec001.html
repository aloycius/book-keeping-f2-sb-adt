Discounts, cash discount and credit terms The term discount refers to a reduction in price that one enjoys while dealing with a business especially in purchasing a product. There are various reasons for which businesses give discounts to customers. These reasons give rise to the three types of discounts, namely quantity, trade and cash discount as follows:

(a) Encouraging purchase on large quantities – in this case, a business may have a policy of giving some price cut (mostly expressed in percentage) to all customers purchasing goods exceeding a certain quantity or a certain value. Such a price cut is called a quantity discount.

(b) Psychological attraction of customers, traders may give price reduction to customers. This may be communicated using price tags that show original price and reduced price (sometimes indicating percentage of price reduction) and thus, attracting customers to the shop. This kind of discount is called a trade discount.

(c) Encouraging credit customers to pay promptly most traders will give credit terms to customers. Credit terms specify the latest date on which the customer must pay the amount due to the trader. However, to encourage early payment, it is common for businesses to offer cash discount. This is the discount that is recorded in the three-column cash book. The quantity and trade

discounts are not recorded in the books of accounts. This is because for those two discounts, the actual price (net of the discount) is the one that is shown in the invoice, which is a source document to record the transactions in the books.

Credit payment terms and cash discount

When goods are sold on credit, the seller has to specify credit terms to the customer. The credit terms specify the date to which the amount on the invoice must be paid. They may also include a cash discount as an incentive to pay before the due date. A cash discount is a reduction from the invoice price when a customer pays within a specified period. There are two main types of credit terms: credit terms without cash discount and credit terms with cash discount.

Credit terms without cash discount

These are credit terms that do not include cash discount. In other words, there is no difference between the customer who pays within a day and the one who pays at the end of the credit period, as long as payment is made within the specified period. The terms can either be stated in a sentence, such as ‘payment within 30 days’ or use abbreviations such as f/30, meaning the full amount is payable within 30 days.
