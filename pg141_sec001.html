(iv) Value-based budgeting. This approach allocates money based on the business’s priorities and long-term financial goals. Businesses do not just continue with the past spending patterns, but determine which areas are most important and allocate resources accordingly. For example, if a company values innovation, it may set aside TZS 5,000,000 for research and development rather than administrative costs. Each budgeting approach serves a specific purpose, and businesses must select the one that aligns with their needs. By choosing the most suitable approach, a business can enhance financial planning, minimise unnecessary expenses, and ensure efficient resource use.

Features of a good budget

A well-prepared budget should have the following characteristics to assist individuals and businesses in managing their finances effectively:

Realistic: A good budget should be based on accurate estimates of revenues and

expenses to avoid financial problems. For example, if a shop owner expects to make TZS 500,000 in monthly sales, they should not budget for expenses of TZS 700,000 because it will lead to a cash shortage. Overestimating cash inflows or underestimating costs can lead to difficulty paying rent, restocking goods, or covering business expenses.

Clear and simple: A budget should be clear and simple; so, everyone involved can easily understand and use it. It should be well-organised, listing revenues and expenditures in a straightforward format to avoid confusion. A simple budget helps individuals or businesses to track finances effectively and make informed decisions. For example, a small shop owner should clearly outline daily sales, rent, and stock purchases. If a budget is too complex, people may find it challenging to use or understand, resulting in poor financial management and unexpected losses.

Flexible: A good budget should allow for adjustments when financial situations change. For example, a farmer planned a budget assuming normal rainfall but later faced drought. In that case, he/she should be able to adjust costs, reducing non- essential expenses or finding alternative income sources. Flexibility ensures that unexpected events, such as emergency repairs or medical bills, do not disrupt financial stability.
