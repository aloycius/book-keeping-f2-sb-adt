Features of a good budget

A well-prepared budget should have
the following characteristics to assist
individuals and businesses in managing
their finances effectively:

Realistic: A good budget should be based on accurate estimates of revenues and

expenses to avoid financial problems.

For example, if a shop owner expects to
make TZS 500,000 in monthly sales, they
should not budget for expenses of TZS

700,000 because it will lead to a cash

shortage. Overestimating cash inflows
or underestimating costs can lead to
difficulty paying rent, restocking goods,
or covering business expenses.

Clear and simple: A budget should be

clear and simple; so, everyone involved
can easily understand and use it. It should
be well-organised, listing revenues and
expenditures in a straightforward format
to avoid confusion. A simple budget helps
individuals or businesses to track finances
effectively and make informed decisions.

For example, a small shop owner should
clearly outline daily sales, rent, and stock
purchases. If a budget is too complex,
people may find it challenging to use or
understand, resulting in poor financial
management and unexpected losses.

Flexible:
A good budget should allow
for adjustments when financial situations
change. For example, a farmer planned a
budget assuming normal rainfall but later
faced drought. In that case, he/she should
be able to adjust costs, reducing non-
essential expenses or finding alternative
income sources. Flexibility ensures that

unexpected events, such as emergency
repairs or medical bills, do not disrupt

financial stability.
