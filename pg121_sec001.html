Reconciling bank account with cash book balances

Exercise 3.6

The manager of Kitambi store has extracted the following details from the comparison of the cash book and bank statement for the month of December 2023. (a) The cash book showed a debit balance of TZS 9,360,000 at the bank, and bank statement showed a credit balance of TZS 5,961,000. (b) Cheque drawn but not yet presented to the bank: Sarinjo TZS 171,000; Hafsa TZS 294,000 (c) Cheque deposited to the bank but not yet credited to the bank: Marwa TZS 600,000; Misuka TZS 165,000 (d) Items shown in the bank statement but not yet entered in the cash book: Standing order for insurance premium TZS 2,520,000; Bank charges TZS 57,000. (e) Cheque received from Songa and dishonoured by the bank TZS 468,000. (f) Cheque payment of TZS 324,000 has been entered in the cash book (bank column) as TZS 270,000.

Required: Assist the manager to work on the following aspects in the reconciliation process:

(i) Draw adjusted cash book (ii) Prepare bank reconciliation statement starting with the adjusted can book balance. (iii) Write bank reconciliation statement without adjusting cash book starting with balance as per cash book.

Reconciliation in overdraft situation There are situations where at the end of the month the business will have an overdrawn balance with the bank. It may happen that both the cash book and the bank statement show an overdraft at the particular date. It may also happen that either the cash book or the bank statement shows an overdraft, while the other shows a favourable balance. Whatever the situation, the bank reconciliation statement can be prepared to reconcile the two balances either by adjusting the cash book or without adjusting the cash book. The approach is the same as learnt for favourable balances, only that it is important to be sure the balances should be treated appropriately as overdrawn balances. This is because the reconciling and adjusting items will have a different effect on overdrafts (negative or unfavourable balance) as compared with the effect they have on favourable balances. Example 3.4 illustrates the reconciliation in overdraft situation.

Example 3.4

The cash book of Zena Kisogo, an executive hairdresser showed on 31 st March 2024 a credit balance of TZS 17,526,000 whereas the bank statement showed a debit balance (overdraft) of TZS 18,905,500. Upon comparing the two balances, the following items were discovered:

(a) Cheques drawn and paid to the creditors but not presented for payment TZS 4,350,000. (b) Cheques received from debtors and deposited to the bank but not credited by the bank until a later date amounted to TZS 5,720,000. (c) Bank charges amounted to TZS 7,800.
