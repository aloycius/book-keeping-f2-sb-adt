Bank reconciliation statement with more items Bank reconciliation statement includes other items, such as errors and dishonoured cheques, when reconciling bank records of a business. Therefore, there is a need to know how to treat such items when adjusting the cash book and preparing a bank reconciliation statement.

In the case of errors, the treatment depends on the kind of error and its effect on the relationship between the cash book balance and the bank statement balance. If the error is in the cash book, it is treated as an adjusting item. It is dealt with when preparing the adjusted cash book. It will be treated as a reconciling item if unadjusted cash book balance is used when preparing the bank reconciliation statement.

On the other hand, if the error is in the bank statement, the usual procedure is to notify the bank so that they can make an entry to reverse the error. Such reversing entry is reflected in the next bank statement. This makes bank reconciliation an important process. While waiting for the rectified bank statement, the amount of the error will be treated as a reconciling item.

As for a dishonoured cheque, it is important to consider two possible sides. One is where the cheque dishonoured is a cheque that the business has received and deposited with its banks. Another side is the case where the cheque is the one that the business has issued to its creditor and for one reason or another it is dishonoured by the bank. Cheques received or issued when dishonoured are further clarified in the following subsections.

Cheque received but dishonoured This is the case where the business has received a cheque from a customer and deposited it with its bank. Apart from noticing that it has not been credited in the bank statement when comparing the bank statement and the cash book, the business would also receive a notification from the bank that the cheque is dishonoured. The cheque will also be returned to the business, which can make further reference from the customer. In any case, the dishonored cheque will be treated as an adjusting item. This is because the cheque will have to be cancelled by the customer, who will issue a fresh cheque to the business for the intended payment. The cash book adjustment necessary for this dishonoured cheque is a credit entry. This is because the amount must have been debited in the cash book when this cheque was originally deposited into the bank.

Cheque issued but dishonoured When the business issued the cheque, it must have credited the cash book. The problem could have started when the payee presented the cheque for payment.
