Time-bound:
To facilitate a consistent
financial review, a budget should be
designed for a defined period, whether
daily, weekly, monthly, or annually. For
instance, a small business owner running
a restaurant should create a monthly
budget to cover all operating expenses,
such as inventory, rent, and salaries,
until the end of the month. Monitoring
financial progress and making necessary
adjustments becomes challenging without
setting a clear time frame.

Goal-oriented: A good budget should align with personal or business financial goals

to

ensure

effective

money

management. For example, a young
entrepreneur who expects to expand a
poultry farm should budget for costs,
such as buying more chicks, vaccines,
and feeds while ensuring enough savings
for future growth. A budget without clear
financial goals may lead to unnecessary
spending, delaying progress towards
achieving essential objectives.

Preparation of a simple cash budget

Managing money effectively is important
for daily operations and long-term success
for businesses. A cash budget helps
business owners to plan for the money
they will receive and the money they will
spend, ensuring they always have enough
to cover important costs.

This section explains how to prepare a
simple cash budget, including steps for
organising cash inflows and outflows,
determining the opening and closing
balances, and making adjustments for
cash shortages.
