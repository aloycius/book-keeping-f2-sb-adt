Correction of Book-keeping errors and the Trial balance

3. Error of principle This kind of error happens when an entry is made to a wrong class of account. This error does not affect trial balance agreement, because a debit entry and a credit entry are made; but one of the entries is made to a wrong class of account. An example of an error of principle is a situation where a shop owner has bought shelves for use in the business and debited the transaction in the purchases account instead of shop furniture account. Doing this will overstate the figure of purchases and understate the figure of non-current assets (furniture) of the business. Although not affecting the trial balance agreement, an error of principle would lead to misleading figures in the financial statements. To discover an error of principle, one can review transactions from source documents to journal and ledger entries.

4. Error of original entry This error occurs when the original journal entry and ledger posting are completed in the appropriate accounts, but the amount recorded is wrong. For example, a cash payment of TZS 100,000 for wages is entered in the journal as TZS 1,000,000 which is then posted to the ledger. This means that the transaction was recorded in the wages account and cash account as TZS 1,000,000 instead of TZS 100,000. The error of original entry would in many cases be a result of additional (or omitted) zero in the figure. It could also occur as a result of transposing two digits in the figure, for example, TZS 980,000 is recorded as TZS 890,000. The trial balance would still agree in this case,

since the problem starts with the original (journal) entry. This means that the same incorrect amount will be used for all accounts affected by the transaction.

5. Error of complete reversal of entries This error occurs when entries are posted in wrong sides of accounts concerned. Reversal of entries occurs when a debit entry is made in the account that should be credited and a credit entry is made in the account should be debited. If this is done, the trial balance will still agree, because total debit entries and balances will be the same as the total credit entries and balances. An example of this is where a receipt of cash TZS 204,000 from a debtor is debited to the trade receivables account (which should have been credited) and credited to cash account (which should have been debited). This can be discovered by tracing the source documents to the journal entries.

6. Compensating errors These are two or more errors whose effects on the credit and debit balances happen to cancel each other. This is a situation when there are errors in the balances of two accounts with the same balances in nature, for example, an undercast of a debit balance and an overcast of another debit balance. An example is where there is an error in balancing accounts, where a rent expense account balance is TZS 45,000 more than it should have been, while at the same time wages expense account has a balance undercasted by TZS 45,000. The trial balance would still agree since the errors cancel each other. Another situation is where there are two accounts with opposite balance in nature.
