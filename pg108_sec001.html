Adjusted cash book and bank reconciliation statement

When managing financial records, it is crucial to ensure that the cash book and bank statement reflect accurate

and consistent balances. However, due to timing differences, errors, and unrecorded transactions, discrepancies often arise between the two records. Once a comparison has been made, an adjustment of the cash book can be done. An adjusted cash book is a cash book that is prepared to ensure the real bank balance is reported. The real bank balance reflects the transactions that the bank has completed on behalf of the account holder such as bank charges. In cases where errors exist in the cash book, the adjustment of the cash book will also include correction of such errors. Adjustment in the cash book will give the adjusted cash book balance.

The adjusted cash book balance is up to date and is reported as a bank balance in financial statements. The main items involved in adjusting the cash book are those that the bank has recorded as direct debits and credits.
