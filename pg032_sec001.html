The following are among the transactions in the business of Ming’o during April 2024:

4/04/2024 Received from Amani, a debtor for TZS 650,000 a cheque for the amount due, net of a 5% cash discount.

Recording transactions in a three- column cash book The cash column and the bank column are used in the same way as in the two-column cash book. The three-column cash book has addition of discount columns. The discount column is used whenever cash (or cheque) is received from a customer within the discount period as per credit terms. The same column is used when the business pays in time to enjoy cash discount.

If the customer makes payment within the discount period, there is a need to record both the receipt of cash and the expense incurred as discount. Similarly, in the books of the buyer, paying within the discount period necessitate recording both the payment of cash and the discount income earned. For the purpose of making the relationship between the discount and the amount paid or received, a discount column is created in the cash book. Assuming that the cash book has a cash column and a bank column for one bank only, the discount column will be the

third. This means that the cash book will have three amount columns, and thus, the name three-column cash book.

The use of discount column happens in the transactions where actual discount was incurred or earned by the business. In such cases, the transaction recorded on the particular date will include recording in the cash or bank column the actual amount paid/ received, and recording in the discount column the amount of discount earned or incurred, as the case may be. Example 1.6 illustrates this process. The two transactions in example 1.6 will be recorded in the books of Ming’o accounts as follows:

Sabuni Traders transaction: In this transaction, the gross amount payable to Sabuni Traders should have been TZS 400,000 that is (TZS 380,000 + 20,000). The amount of TZS 380,000 paid is net of the TZS 20,000 cash discount. This means that recording this transaction involves recording both the cash paid and the discount that Ming’o enjoyed. The TZS 20,000 is therefore a discount received.
