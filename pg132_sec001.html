Chapter summary

1. Bank reconciliation statement is a document that provides comparison

of the balances in a business cash

book with the balance in the bank

statement through identifying and

correcting any difference.

2. Bank statements refer to statements

that show transactions between the

bank and its customers for a particular

period as recorded by the bank.

3.
Inconsistencies between the cash

book and bank statement balance can

be caused by unpresented cheques,

uncredited cheques, dishonoured
cheques, bank charges, standing

orders, direct deposits and transfers,
and errors.

4. Adjusted cash book is a cash book that

incorporates transactions undertaken

directly through the bank account

to ensure the real bank balance is

reported.

5. Bank reconciliation is important for

identifying errors in transactions,

checking against fraud that might

be committed by unfaithful bank

cashiers, and tracking receipts and

expenses.

6. The use of software packages such
as Excel in business operations can

help to simplify the task of preparing

the bank reconciliation statement.
