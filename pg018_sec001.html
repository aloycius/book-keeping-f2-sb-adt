Two-column cash book

A cash book with two amount columns

is called a two-column (double column)

cash book. It is called two-column cash

book because it has two amount columns

on each side, one column records cash

transactions, and the other column records

bank transactions.

The bank column records transactions

made through cheques, cash deposits and
other transfers of funds into the bank. On

the other hand, the cash column records

receipts and payments made using physical

cash, cash deposits and cash withdrawals
from bank.

Usefulness of a two-column cash

book

The two-column cash book is useful

because:

(a) It makes it easier to monitor cash

transactions because all the cash flow

is viewed on the same place.

(b) The business manager can see the

cash and bank balances at any point in

time and make appropriate decision.

This can be possible with the use of
technology, for example, computer
systems in preparing a two-column
cash book.

Preparation of a two-column cash book

This cash book operates in the same

way that the cash account and the bank

account would separately operate. The

only difference is that two separate amount

columns are used, on the debit side and on

the credit side. The cash column is used

for cash transactions and the bank column

is used for recording bank transactions.

The other columns remain the same as in
separate cash/ bank accounts or any other
ledger account.
