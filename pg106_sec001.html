It is very important to recognise the difference between the two in terms of identifying them from the records, and making appropriate treatment for each when performing bank reconciliation.

Bank charges These charges would not be recorded in the cash book until the business receives the bank statement, in which case a debit entry for the charges will be observed.

Standing orders Payments through standing orders will appear only in the bank statements at the time they are made by the bank. They would not be reflected in the cash book until the business confirms their execution through receiving the bank statement or any other way of notification that the business considers formal and acceptable for recording transactions in its books.

Direct deposits and transfers (direct credit) These will result in a difference between the balances when such deposits or

transfers are made, and the business has not received a bank statement and updated the cash book.

Errors Errors could occur either in posting transactions to the cash book or bank statement. These would lead to difference between the two records, unless there is a coincidence that the same error is made in both the bank statement and the cash book.

Key terms used in bank reconciliation process Key terms provide various perceptions in the bank reconciliation process. Many key terms are directly linked to specific inconsistencies between cash book and bank statement. These key terms describe concepts used in reconciling bank account and cash book balances. The following table summarises the key terms used in the bank reconciliation process.

Key term Definition Related inconsistency
Outstanding Cheques (unpresented cheques) Cheques issued but not yet presented to the bank or cleared by the bank Cheques recorded in the cash book but not yet debited in the bank statement
Deposits in transit Deposits recorded in the cash book but not yet reflected in the bank statement either in the form of cash or uncredited cheques Delay in processing deposits, causing a difference between the two records
Bank charges Fees deducted by the bank for various services offered by the bank to the customers Charges deducted by the bank but not yet recorded in the cash book
