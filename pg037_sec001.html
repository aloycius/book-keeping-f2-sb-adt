Petty cash book

The word petty means small. A petty cash
book is a separate cash book specifically
maintained to record small cash payments,
also known as petty payments. This book
is usually operated by a specific cashier
known as petty cashier. The petty cash
book records petty payments such as
office expenses, postage, telephone,
stationeries, cleaning expenses, sundry
expenses using columns that are designed
to each category of expenses that the
business may pay for using the petty
cash fund. Petty cash cannot be used to
purchase non-current assets, because
they usually require large amount. When
preparing the petty cash system, the
business would normally have a policy
that states which types of payments are
settled with petty cash fund. The policy
should state the limit of the amount of any
single payment to be made through petty
cash as well as the petty cash period. The
petty cash period is the period between

receiving the petty cash float and petty
cash replenishment.

The imprest system

The petty cash system works where
the main cashier delegates petty cash
payments to the petty cashier. The petty
cashier is therefore provided with an
agreed amount of cash known as petty
cash float for the purpose of making petty
cash payments. The cashier continues
to use the petty cash for making various
payments and records the transactions
until the end of the period, or until the
amount is finished. At the end of this,
the petty cashier submits the details of
payments and relevant source documents
to the main cashier. The main cashier then
makes a replenishment of the amount
spent so that a new petty cash period can
begin. The replenishment of the petty
cash float is known as reimbursement.

This kind of operation is known as an
imprest system, and the petty cash float is
also referred to as imprest amount.
