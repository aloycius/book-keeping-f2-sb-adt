Uncredited cheques

An uncredited cheque is a cheque which
the business has received and deposited
into the bank, but the bank has not yet
completed clearing the cheque with the
drawer’s bank. The amount must have
been debited in the cash book when the
cheque was lodged to the bank, but will
not appear on the credit column of the
bank statement until when the respective
cheque is cleared. Uncredited cheques are
also called uncleared cheques, uncleared
deposits or deposits in transit.

Dishonoured cheques

A dishonoured cheque is a cheque that
the bank refuses to pay (to honour) for
various reasons. Cheques are dishonored
for the following reasons:

(a) The drawer has insufficient funds
with the bank, for example, the
drawer has a bank balance of TZS

1,240,000 and has issued a cheque
for TZS 1,500,000.

(b) The cheque has an error(s) on it, for
example amount in words differs
from the amount in figures.

(c) It is not signed by the drawer, or
the signature does not match the
signature in the bank records.

(d) If the payee has not signed the back
of the cheque (endorsed) before
presenting it to the bank. For some

customers, the bank may want the
payee or the drawer to stamp on the
back of the cheque.

(e) It is post-dated. A post-dated cheque is presented for payment before the issue date (date written on the cheque). An example of a post-dated cheque is a cheque that the date of drawing or issue is written as 1st June

2023 but presented for payment on

31st May 2023.

(f) It is stale. A stale cheque is a cheque that is presented for payment at a date later than six months from the issue date or from the date it was drawn. An example of a stale cheque is a cheque drawn on 20th September

2023 but presented to the bank for payment on 1st April 2024.

Any dishonoured cheque would have
already been recorded in the cash book, but
not in the bank statement. It will therefore
cause a difference between a cash book
balance and the bank statement balance,
until the business becomes aware that the
cheque is dishonoured and reflect them
in the cash book. When you are doing
business and operate a current account,
dishonoured cheques may happen in two
sides, namely:

(i)

Cheques that the business has

received and deposited to the bank

but dishonoured by the drawer’s

bank(s); and

(ii)

Cheques that the business has issued

to different payees and dishonoured

by its bank.
