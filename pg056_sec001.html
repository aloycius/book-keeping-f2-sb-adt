Book-keeping errors that do not affect
the trial balance agreement

A trial balance agrees when the total of
debit balances equals the total of credit
balances. The trial balance will agree
even if some types of book-keeping errors
exist in the books of accounts, provided
that the total value of debit entries made
is equal to the total value of credit entries
made. This also happens whenever the
total of credit balances listed in the trial
balance is equal to the total of debit
balances, irrespective of the correctness
of such balances. Therefore, trial balance
will agree even if there is existence of
one or more errors because:

(i)

There is an error that affects both a

debit balance and a credit balance

by the same amount, or

(ii) There is an error in which an entry

has not been recored in the books

of accounts for that particular

transaction, or

(iii)
There are two independent errors that

cancel each other (with equal amount

on debit and credit side).

Considering these situations, there are six
types of book-keeping errors that do not
affect the trial balance agreement. These
are:

1. Error of omission

This is an error which occurs when the
transaction is not recorded in the books
of accounts. For example, a purchase
of goods for cash TZS 345,000 is not
recorded in the books of accounts. This
transaction should have been recorded by
making a debit entry of TZS 345,000 in

the purchases account and a credit entry
of TZS 345,000 in the cash account.

When this is not done, the trial balance
will agree because the entries are omitted
from both the purchases account (debit
side) and the cash account (credit side).

This kind of error can be detected by
tracing source documents (in this case
the cash payment vouchers) to the journal
and the ledger.

2. Error of commission

This is an error in which an entry is made to
a wrong personal account that tends to be
in the same class of account as the correct
account, in which it should have been
made. An example of this error happens
in either the sales ledger or purchases
ledger, in which a transaction is entered
in a wrong personal account of a debtor
or creditor, especially when the names of
such persons have some similarities. For
example, a TZS 250,000 sales of goods
to Hawa Mwalimu has been debited in
the account of Hawa Maalim, who also
happens to be in the sales ledger of the
business. This transaction was supposed
to be debited in Hawa Mwalimu’s
account, but was posted to Hawa Maalim.

The posting to the general ledger, in this
case, the trade receivables account and
the sales account will not be affected by
this error. The trial balance will therefore,
agree even if this error has been made in
the subsidiary ledger accounts of Hawa

Mwalimu and Hawa Maalim. This error
can be discovered by vouching subsidiary
ledger entries to special journal entries
and to the source documents and
confirm whether the names in the source
documents match with those in the books.
