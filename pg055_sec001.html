Correction of Book-keeping errors and the Trial balance

wrong computation of ledger balances,

or even wrong preparation of the trial

balance. The effect of book-keeping errors

is that the affected account balances will

not reflect the transactions that have taken

place in the business during the period.

At the end of the financial year, adjustments

are made to the balances for some items,

after which, the balances are used in

the preparation of financial statements.

When book-keeping errors occur at any

stage, the resulting financial statements

will be misleading, if such errors are not

corrected. The causes of book-keeping

errors can be summarised as follows:

(i) Failure to record the correct amount
from source documents.

(ii) Omitting some items from the

source documents when entering

transactions in the books of prime

entry.

(iii) Making entries to a wrong account.

(iv) Failure to make appropriate

application of the double entry

principle.

(v)
Making arithmetical mistakes

when summing up amounts in

special journals/ daybooks or when

calculating balances for different

ledger accounts.

Sources of book-keeping errors in

the trial balance

These refer to the causes of errors that
occurs in the process of preparing a trial

balance. These causes often arise due to

human mistakes, system problems and
procedural weakness. The following are
examples of sources of errors occurring
in the trial balance:

Lack of accounting knowledge
: the book-
keeper may lack or may have insufficient
accounting knowledge and skills leading
to recording incorrect figure.

Clerical mistakes:
these may occur due to
incorrect totalling or transferring balances
during recording of transactions.

Carelessness:
this occurs when book-
keeper enter wrong figures, skipping
transactions and reverse entries when
preparing the trial balance.

Technical errors:
these arise due to
problems in accounting software or
system failures leading to miscalculations.

Fraud or intentional errors:
these occurs
when a person deliberately manipulates
records when extracting the trial balance
to hide financial information.

Types of book-keeping errors

There are two broad categories of errors

which are grouped depending on how

they affect the trial balance agreement.

These categories are:

(i)
Errors that do not affect the trial balance

agreement.

(ii) Errors that affect the trial balance

agreement.

For the purpose of making appropriate correction of errors, it may be useful to understand the type of error in each

categories.
